## Apps

An app is one of this CRM's work areas — Contacts, CRM, Actions,
Definitions, Settings, or this Help page — grouped by the kind of work it's
for. The apps page is just a launcher; nothing is stored here.

## Partners

A partner is a person or a company you've confirmed is real — the record
this CRM treats as your actual customer or prospect. A person can sit under
their company.

## Leads

A lead is someone you might contact who hasn't been confirmed yet — a name
and a phone number is enough to get started. A lead isn't on the pipeline
board, and creating one doesn't create a partner.

## Deals

A deal is a lead you've decided to pursue. It belongs to one partner and
one service, and it moves through the pipeline toward Won or Lost. In the
database this is called an opportunity; on screen it's called a deal.

## Pipeline

The pipeline is the board view of your open deals, grouped into columns by
stage. Drag a deal from one column to another to move it forward. Lost
deals drop off the board, and leads never appear here.

## Campaigns

A campaign is one planned push: a single audience, one offer, and one
opening line for the call. Deals and leads join a campaign by carrying its
tag, and the campaign page shows how far along it is against its targets.

## Calls

The call list is today's queue of deals worth phoning, ranked by a score,
with one-tap buttons for the outcome of each call. The score comes from the
deal's value, where the contact came from, how complete its details are,
and how recently the stage changed — it's a formula, not a prediction, and
it doesn't decide who you should call.

## Services

A service is one of the things you sell. Every deal and every offer is
attached to one service.

## Stages

A stage is one column on the pipeline board, marking where a deal is in
your process — for example New, Contacted, or Won. A few special roles
tell the CRM which stage new deals start in, which stage feeds the call
list, and which stages close a deal.

## Offers

An offer is the price and the short pitch for a service that you use on a
call — what you actually say and charge. A service can have several
offers; one is marked as the default a new deal starts on.

## Automations

An automation is an optional rule that creates a task or a follow-on deal
automatically when a deal enters a chosen stage. A brand-new CRM has none
set up — you add them when you want something to happen on its own.

## Lost reasons

A lost reason is the explanation you pick when you stop pursuing a deal —
for example "Went with a competitor" or "Not now." One reason, "Not now,"
can also notify your nurture tool automatically, if you've set one up.
A reason marked as a disqualification (the wrong kind of business, too
small, out of area) shows the deal as disqualified rather than lost.

## ICP

ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) fit criteria are the rules this CRM uses to
score how well a lead or deal matches the kind of customer you actually
want. Each matching rule adds the points you gave it — there's no pass or
fail line, you or your agent decide what score is good enough. A rule can
also check a tag: the company's tags, or the lead's own tags. One rule per
tag, and those points add up with the rest.

## Settings

Settings is where you manage your own password, your currency, and the
keys an agent or an import uses to connect to this CRM. There's no team
management here — this CRM is built for one person logged in.
